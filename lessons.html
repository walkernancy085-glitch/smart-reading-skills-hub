LESSON 1

Understanding What You Read

Reading comprehension is the ability to understand, think about and respond to information in a text. Good readers do more than pronounce words correctly. They think about the meaning of the words, sentences and ideas they read.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video carefully before answering the questions.

🎯 Learning Goal

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify important information and answer questions about a passage.

📌 Remember

Always use information from the passage when answering comprehension questions.

🔑 Key Questions

Who? What? Where? When? Why? How?

What Does a Good Reader Do?

A good reader thinks before, during and after reading. Before reading, the reader predicts what the text may be about. During reading, the reader looks for important information. After reading, the reader checks whether the text was understood.

Before Reading

  • Read the title.
  • Look at pictures or headings.
  • Think about what you already know.
  • Predict what the text may be about.

During Reading

  • Read each paragraph carefully.
  • Look for important information.
  • Notice unfamiliar words.
  • Ask questions about what you are reading.

After Reading

  • Think about the main idea.
  • Remember important details.
  • Answer questions using evidence.
  • Summarise what you have read.

📖 Reading Example

Ama visited the community library after school. She wanted to borrow a storybook for her reading practice. The librarian showed her several books and helped her choose one about animals.

Ama sat quietly and read the first few pages. When she found a word she did not understand, she looked at the sentences around it for clues. She then checked the word in a dictionary.

Check Your Understanding

1. Where did Ama go after school?

Answer: She went to the community library.

2. Why did she visit the library?

Answer: She wanted to borrow a storybook for reading practice.

3. What did Ama do when she found a difficult word?

Answer: She looked for clues in the surrounding sentences and then checked a dictionary.

⭐ Reading Tip: If you are not sure about an answer, go back to the passage. The text may contain a clue that helps you.

🎯 Quick Challenge

Read a short paragraph from your textbook. Then identify:

  • one important person or character;
  • one important event;
  • one important detail;
  • the main idea.
LESSON 2

Understanding New Words

Vocabulary refers to the words we know, understand and use. A strong vocabulary helps us understand what we read and express our thoughts clearly.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video carefully and learn how to use context clues to understand unfamiliar words.

🎯 Learning Goal

Learn ways to work out the meanings of unfamiliar words.

🔎 Context Clues

Use words and sentences around an unfamiliar word to find clues.

📚 Dictionary

Use a dictionary to check meaning, spelling and pronunciation.

Ways to Learn a New Word

1. Use Context Clues

Look at the words around the unfamiliar word. They may give you a clue about its meaning.

2. Look for Synonyms

A synonym is a word with a similar meaning. For example, large and big.

3. Look for Antonyms

An antonym is a word with an opposite meaning. For example, early and late.

4. Use a Dictionary

A dictionary can provide the meaning, spelling and pronunciation of a word.

5. Look at Word Parts

Prefixes, suffixes and root words can sometimes help us understand the meaning of unfamiliar words.

📖 Example

The children were excited when they heard that their school would visit the zoo. They talked happily about the animals they expected to see.

The word excited means feeling very happy and enthusiastic about something.

The second sentence gives us a clue because the children were talking happily about the visit.

🔑 Word Practice

Happy

Synonym: joyful

Begin

Synonym: start

Quiet

Antonym: noisy

🎯 Quick Challenge

Read this sentence:

"The road was narrow, so only one car could pass through at a time."

What does narrow mean in this sentence?

Use the sentence to help you work out the meaning.

⭐ Vocabulary Tip: Do not immediately skip a difficult word. First, try to understand it from the sentence and surrounding information.
LESSON 3

Finding the Most Important Idea

The main idea is the most important point the writer wants the reader to understand. Supporting details provide information, examples or facts that explain the main idea.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video and identify the nouns, verbs and adjectives.

🎯 Main Idea

What the paragraph or passage is mostly about.

🧩 Supporting Details

Information that explains or supports the main idea.

❓ Key Question

What is the writer mainly telling me?

How to Find the Main Idea

  1. Read the whole paragraph.
  2. Identify the topic.
  3. Look for the most important message.
  4. Separate important ideas from small details.
  5. Say the main idea in your own words.

📖 Example Passage

The school garden provides vegetables for the school. Learners water the plants every morning. They remove weeds and protect the plants from animals. They also make sure the soil remains healthy.

Main Idea: The school garden provides vegetables and is cared for by the learners.

Supporting Details:

  • Learners water the plants.
  • They remove weeds.
  • They protect the plants.
  • They care for the soil.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Do not choose a small detail as the main idea. A supporting detail is only one part of the paragraph. The main idea covers the whole paragraph.

🎯 Quick Challenge

Read this paragraph:

"Regular exercise helps people stay healthy. Walking, running and playing games can help strengthen the body. Exercise can also help people feel active and energetic."

Question: What is the main idea?

Think: Which sentence or idea covers the whole paragraph?

⭐ Reading Tip: Ask yourself: "What is this paragraph mostly about?"
LESSON 4

Reading Between the Lines

Sometimes a writer does not tell us everything directly. Readers must use clues from the text and what they already know to understand information that is not directly stated.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video and learn how to write complete sentences.

🧠 Inference

An idea you understand by combining text clues with what you already know.

🔮 Prediction

What you think may happen next based on clues in the text.

🔎 Evidence

Information from the passage that supports your thinking.

How to Make an Inference

  1. Find clues in the passage.
  2. Think about what you already know.
  3. Put the information together.
  4. Make a reasonable conclusion.
  5. Explain which clues helped you.

📖 Example

Kofi entered the house carrying an umbrella. His clothes were wet and water was dripping from his shoes.

Inference: It probably rained outside.

Evidence: Kofi had an umbrella, wet clothes and wet shoes.

Prediction: Kofi may change his wet clothes.

Inference or Prediction?

Inference

The character is probably tired because she has been walking for several hours.

Prediction

She may rest when she reaches home.

🎯 Quick Challenge

"Yaw looked at the dark clouds and quickly closed all the windows. He picked up the clothes that were drying outside."

What can you infer?

What do you predict may happen next?

⭐ Thinking Tip: A good inference should have evidence. Do not make a random guess. Use clues from the text.
LESSON 5

Understanding Order and Relationships

Sequencing means identifying the order in which events happen. Cause and effect explains why something happens and what happens as a result.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video carefully. Learn how to use first, next, then, and finally to show the order in which events happen.

1️⃣ Sequence

Shows the order in which events happen.

🔗 Cause

Explains why something happened.

➡️ Effect

Explains what happened as a result.

Sequence Signal Words

First → Next → Then → After that → Finally

Cause and Effect Signal Words

because, so, therefore, as a result, since

📖 Example

First, Kojo planted the seeds. Next, he watered them. After several days, the seeds began to grow.

Sequence: Plant → Water → Grow

Cause: Kojo watered the seeds.

Effect: The seeds received water and began to grow.

🎯 Quick Challenge

Put these events in the correct order:

A

The learner submitted the work.

B

The learner read the instructions.

C

The learner completed the work.

Think carefully about what would normally happen first, next and last.

⭐ Reading Tip: When reading a story, pay attention to time-order words. They help you understand what happened first, next and finally.
LESSON 6

Strategies for Better Reading

Reading strategies are methods that help readers understand a text before, during and after reading.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video carefully and learn useful reading strategies to use before, during, and after reading.

👀 Preview

Look at the title, pictures, headings and layout before reading.

❓ Question

Ask questions about what you expect to learn.

🔮 Predict

Think about what the text may discuss.

📝 Summarise

Explain the most important ideas after reading.

Before Reading

  • Read the title.
  • Look at pictures.
  • Read headings.
  • Think about what you already know.
  • Make a prediction.

During Reading

  • Ask questions.
  • Look for important information.
  • Check unfamiliar words.
  • Stop and think about each paragraph.
  • Reread difficult sections.

After Reading

  • Identify the main idea.
  • Recall important details.
  • Answer questions.
  • Summarise the text.
  • Think about what you learnt.

📖 Example

Imagine you are about to read a passage titled "How Plants Grow."

Before reading, you could ask:

  • What do plants need to grow?
  • What do I already know about plants?
  • What will I learn from this passage?

After reading, you could check whether your questions were answered.

⭐ Strategy Tip: Good readers do not read passively. They interact with the text by asking questions, making predictions and checking their understanding.
LESSON 7

Writing a Good Summary

📖 What Is a Summary?

A summary is a short version of a longer passage, story or text. It gives the most important ideas without including every detail.

A good summary helps a reader understand what the text is mainly about without reading the whole text.

⭐ What Should a Good Summary Include?

A good summary should:

  • Give the main idea of the text.
  • Include the most important points.
  • Use your own words.
  • Be shorter than the original text.
  • Be clear and easy to understand.
  • Leave out unnecessary details.

📝 Five Steps for Writing a Summary

Step 1 – Read the text

Read the passage carefully so that you understand what it is about.

Step 2 – Find the main idea

Ask yourself: What is the most important thing the writer wants me to know?

Step 3 – Find the important points

Pick out the important facts, events or ideas that support the main idea.

Step 4 – Write in your own words

Put the important ideas together using your own words. Do not copy the whole passage.

Step 5 – Check your summary

Read your summary again. Make sure it is clear, short and contains the important ideas.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video and learn five simple steps for writing a good summary.

💡 Example

The School Garden

The pupils in Class Five started a small garden behind their classroom. They planted tomatoes, pepper and onions. Every morning, some pupils watered the plants while others removed weeds. After several weeks, the plants grew well and produced vegetables. The pupils were happy because they had worked together to care for the garden.

Important Points
  • Class Five started a school garden.
  • They planted vegetables.
  • The pupils cared for the plants.
  • The plants grew and produced vegetables.
  • The pupils worked together.
Good Summary

Class Five pupils started a garden and planted vegetables. They worked together to water and care for the plants until they produced vegetables.

⭐ Remember!

A summary tells the main idea and the most important points in a shorter form.

Read → Find the main idea → Pick important points → Use your own words → Check

LESSON 8

Reading Smoothly, Accurately and with Expression

Reading fluency means reading accurately, smoothly and with appropriate expression. Fluent readers recognise words correctly and read in a way that makes the meaning clear.

🎥 Watch and Learn

Watch the video carefully. Learn how fluent readers read accurately, at an appropriate rate, and with expression.

✓ Accuracy

Read words correctly.

⏱ Pace

Read at a comfortable speed without rushing.

🎙 Expression

Use your voice to show meaning and feeling.

Why Is Fluency Important?

When reading becomes smoother, the reader can focus more attention on understanding the meaning of the text.

How to Improve Fluency

1. Read Silently

Read the passage first to understand its meaning.

2. Read Aloud

Practise saying the words clearly.

3. Notice Punctuation

Pause appropriately at commas and full stops.

4. Read Again

Repeated reading can improve smoothness and confidence.

📖 Reading Expression

"Watch out!" shouted Kojo.

"What happened?" asked Ama.

"The ball is coming towards us!" Kojo replied.

When reading this dialogue, your voice should show the difference between a warning, a question and a reply.

🎯 Fluency Practice

  1. Choose a short passage.
  2. Read it silently.
  3. Read it aloud slowly.
  4. Read it again with better expression.
  5. Ask yourself whether your reading sounded smooth and clear.
⭐ Fluency Tip: Do not rush just to finish the passage. Read at a comfortable pace that allows you to understand what you are saying.
LESSON 9

Understanding Different Types of Texts

📖 What Is a Text?

A text is any piece of spoken or written language that communicates information, ideas, instructions, opinions, feelings or stories.

We read different types of texts every day. We may read a story, a newspaper article, a recipe, instructions, an advertisement or an information article.

Different texts have different purposes. Knowing the type and purpose of a text helps us understand what we are reading.

🎯 What You Will Learn

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain what a text is.
  • Identify different types of texts.
  • Explain the purpose of each type of text.
  • Identify examples of different text types.
  • Use clues to identify the type of a text.

⭐ Five Common Types of Texts

In this lesson, we will learn about five useful types of texts.

1. Narrative Text 📚

A narrative text tells a story. It usually has characters, a setting and events.

The events may be real or imaginary.

Examples: short stories, folktales, novels and fables.

Purpose: To tell a story or entertain the reader.

2. Informational Text 🔎

An informational text gives facts and information about a subject.

It helps the reader learn about a topic.

Examples: textbooks, reports, articles and information pages.

Purpose: To inform or teach the reader.

3. Persuasive Text 💬

A persuasive text tries to convince the reader to agree with an idea or take an action.

The writer may give reasons to support an opinion.

Examples: advertisements, opinion pieces, persuasive letters and campaign messages.

Purpose: To persuade or convince the reader.

4. Instructional Text 📝

An instructional text gives directions or steps for doing something.

The steps are usually arranged in an order that the reader should follow.

Examples: recipes, rules, manuals and directions.

Purpose: To tell the reader how to do something.

5. Descriptive Text 🌳

A descriptive text gives details about a person, place, animal, object or experience.

It often uses adjectives and other descriptive words to help the reader form a clear picture.

Examples: descriptions of people, places, animals and objects.

Purpose: To describe something clearly.

🧠 Easy Way to Remember

Narrative → tells a story

Informational → gives facts

Persuasive → tries to convince

Instructional → gives steps

Descriptive → gives details

🔍 How to Identify a Text Type

When you read a text, ask yourself: “Why was this text written?”

Ask Question 1

Does the text tell a story with characters and events?

If yes, it is probably a narrative text .

Ask Question 2

Does the text give facts or information about a topic?

If yes, it is probably an informational text .

Ask Question 3

Is the writer trying to make you agree or take an action?

If yes, it is probably a persuasive text .

Ask Question 4

Does the text give steps or directions?

If yes, it is probably an instructional text .

Ask Question 5

Does the text give many details about something?

If yes, it is probably a descriptive text .

💡 Example 1 – Narrative Text

The Lost Book

Kojo was getting ready for school when he noticed that his English book was missing. He searched under his bed and inside his school bag, but he could not find it. His sister helped him search the room. Finally, Kojo found the book on a chair in the sitting room.

Text type: Narrative

Why? It tells a story about a character and events.

Plants need water to grow and stay healthy. Water helps plants transport nutrients from the soil. Without enough water, plants may become weak and dry.

Text type: Informational

Why? It gives facts about plants and water.

💡 Example 3 – Persuasive Text

Read Every Day

Everyone should spend some time reading every day. Reading helps us learn new words and improve our understanding. Make reading part of your daily routine!

Text type: Persuasive

Why? The writer is trying to convince readers to read every day.

💡 Example 4 – Instructional Text

How to Wash Your Hands

First, wet your hands with clean water. Next, apply soap and rub your hands together. Clean between your fingers and under your nails. Finally, rinse your hands and dry them.

Text type: Instructional

Why? It gives steps for completing an activity.

💡 Example 5 – Descriptive Text

A Beautiful Garden

The garden was bright and colourful. Tall green plants stood beside small red flowers. A gentle breeze moved the leaves, while the sweet smell of flowers filled the air.

Text type: Descriptive

Why? It gives details that help the reader imagine the garden.

Community Reading Club

Every Saturday morning, the community library became a lively place as children arrived for their reading club. Some carried storybooks from home, while others borrowed books from the library shelves. The members greeted one another and arranged their chairs in a circle before the session began.

Their teacher, Madam Esi, divided the children into small groups. Each group received a different story to read. The children took turns reading aloud while the others listened carefully. Whenever a reader came across an unfamiliar word, the group discussed its meaning and tried to use it in a sentence.

One Saturday, Adwoa brought a storybook about a young girl who helped her community solve a problem. After reading the story, the group discussed the character's actions. Kojo thought the girl was courageous, while Adwoa believed that her willingness to listen to others was her greatest strength.

Before the meeting ended, Madam Esi asked the children what they had gained from the club. Several pupils said that they had become more confident readers. Others said they had learned new words and discovered interesting books.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. Why did the children meet at the community library?
  2. How did the groups deal with unfamiliar words?
  3. What different opinions did Kojo and Adwoa express?
  4. What benefits did the children gain from the reading club?

Vocabulary: lively, unfamiliar, courageous, confidence, experiences

The Cocoa Farmer's Morning

Kofi woke up before sunrise because he was going to the cocoa farm with his father. After eating breakfast, they gathered their tools and walked along the quiet path leading to the farm. Kofi was excited because he wanted to learn more about cocoa farming.

When they arrived, Kofi noticed that some weeds had grown around the cocoa trees. His father explained that weeds compete with cocoa trees for water and nutrients. Together, they carefully removed the weeds and cleared the area around the trees.

Later, Kofi's father showed him how ripe cocoa pods were identified. He explained that farmers must harvest carefully so that the trees are not damaged. Kofi listened closely and helped collect the harvested pods.

As they prepared to return home, Kofi felt tired but satisfied. He had learned that farming required patience, hard work and careful planning. His father also explained that cocoa farming provides income for many families.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. Why did Kofi go to the cocoa farm?
  2. Why were the weeds removed?
  3. What did Kofi's father teach him about harvesting?
  4. What did Kofi learn about farming?

Vocabulary: nutrients, harvesting, accomplished, patience, income

Ama's Visit to the Market

Ama accompanied her mother to the market on Saturday morning. They planned to buy tomatoes, onions, vegetables, fish and a few other items needed for the week. The market was already crowded when they arrived.

Ama was fascinated by the different sounds and activities around her. Traders called out to customers while people moved between the stalls. Her mother reminded her to stay close because it would be easy to become separated in the busy market.

When they reached the vegetable section, Ama noticed that the price of tomatoes had increased. The seller explained that there had been less tomatoes available that week because of poor weather. Ama listened carefully as her mother discussed the price with the seller.

Before leaving, Ama noticed an elderly woman struggling to carry her belongings. Ama offered to help her carry one of the bags. Her mother smiled and praised her for being thoughtful and helpful.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. What items did Ama and her mother plan to buy?
  2. Why did Ama's mother tell her to stay close?
  3. Why had the price of tomatoes changed?
  4. How did Ama help the elderly woman?

Vocabulary: crowded, fascinated, availability, demand, belongings

The Homowo Celebration

The community was preparing for the Homowo celebration. For several weeks, families had been cleaning their homes and surroundings. Traders were also preparing food and other items for the celebration. The children were excited because they knew the festival would bring families and friends together.

On the morning of the celebration, the streets became lively. People dressed in colourful clothes and prepared traditional foods. Drumming, singing and dancing could be heard in different parts of the community. The children watched with excitement as the elders explained some of the customs connected with the celebration.

Ama's family prepared food to share with relatives and visitors. Before eating, her grandmother explained that Homowo was a time to remember the history and experiences of the Ga people. Ama listened carefully because she wanted to understand why the celebration was important to her community.

Later in the day, Ama joined other children in the celebration. She enjoyed the music and activities, but she also remembered what her grandmother had taught her. She realised that festivals were not only opportunities to have fun but also ways of preserving culture, history and community values.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. How did the community prepare for the Homowo celebration?
  2. What activities did the children observe during the celebration?
  3. What did Ama's grandmother teach her about Homowo?
  4. Why are festivals important to communities?

Vocabulary: celebration, customs, relatives, culture, preserving

The Helpful Classmate

Sena was reading a passage during an English lesson, but she found it difficult to understand the second paragraph. She read it several times but still could not identify the main idea.

Her classmate Yaw noticed that she was struggling. Instead of giving her the answer, he asked her to read the paragraph again and underline the important words. He then encouraged her to think about what those words had in common.

Sena followed Yaw's advice. After looking carefully at the important words, she was able to identify the main idea of the paragraph. She then explained her answer in her own words.

Sena felt more confident because she had solved the problem herself. Yaw was happy that he had helped her understand the reading strategy rather than simply giving her the answer.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. Why was Sena having difficulty?
  2. What strategy did Yaw use to help her?
  3. Why did Yaw avoid giving Sena the answer?
  4. How did Sena feel after completing the second paragraph?

Vocabulary: guidance, strategy, confident, identify, paragraph

Protecting Our Environment

The pupils noticed that some areas around their school were becoming untidy. Plastic bags and empty bottles were sometimes left on the ground after break time. The pupils were concerned because the rubbish made the school environment unpleasant.

They also noticed that a drainage channel near the school had become blocked with rubbish. Their teacher explained that a blocked drainage channel could prevent water from flowing properly and might cause flooding when it rained heavily.

The pupils decided to take action. They organised a clean-up activity and placed waste into the appropriate bins. They also created simple posters reminding other pupils not to litter.

After the activity, the school environment looked cleaner. The pupils understood that protecting the environment was not only the responsibility of adults. Everyone could contribute by keeping the surroundings clean and using school facilities responsibly.

Mini Reading Lesson

  1. What problems did the pupils notice?
  2. Why was the blocked drainage channel a concern?
  3. What actions did the pupils take?
  4. What can you do to protect your environment?

Vocabulary: environment, drainage, responsible, facilities, campaign
