LESSON 1

Understanding What You Read

Reading comprehension means understanding the meaning of a text. A good reader does not only read the words. The reader also thinks about the information in the text.

What to do

Read the passage carefully. Think about the characters, events, places and important information.

Ask yourself

Who? What? Where? When? Why? How?

Example

Ama visited the community library after school. She borrowed a storybook about Ghanaian wildlife.

Question: Where did Ama go after school?

Answer: She went to the community library.

Reading Tip: Look back at the passage when you are not sure about an answer.

LESSON 2

Understanding New Words

Vocabulary refers to the words we know and understand. Good vocabulary helps us understand passages and express our ideas clearly.

Use the context

Look at the words and sentences around an unfamiliar word for clues about its meaning.

Use a dictionary

A dictionary can help you find the meaning, pronunciation and spelling of a word.

Example

The children were excited when they heard that their school would visit the zoo.

Excited means feeling very happy and enthusiastic about something.

Vocabulary Tip: When you meet a new word, try to guess its meaning before checking the dictionary.

LESSON 3

Finding the Main Idea

The main idea is the most important point or message in a paragraph or passage. Supporting details give information that explains or supports the main idea.

Main Idea

The main point the writer wants the reader to understand.

Supporting Details

Facts, examples and information that explain the main idea.

Example

The school garden provides vegetables for the school. Learners water the plants every morning. They also remove weeds and protect the plants from animals.

Main Idea: The school garden provides vegetables for the school.

Supporting Details: Learners water the plants, remove weeds and protect the plants.

Reading Tip: Ask yourself, "What is this paragraph mostly about?"
LESSON 4

Reading Between the Lines

An inference is an idea we understand from clues in a text even when the writer does not state it directly. A prediction is what we think may happen next.

Inference

Use information from the text and your own knowledge to understand something that is not directly stated.

Prediction

Use clues from the text to decide what may happen next.

Example

Kofi entered the house carrying an umbrella. His clothes were wet and water was dripping from his shoes.

Inference: It probably rained outside.

Prediction: Kofi may change his wet clothes.

Thinking Tip: Always use clues from the passage to support your inference or prediction.
LESSON 5

Understanding Order and Relationships

Sequencing means identifying the order in which events happen. Cause and effect explains why something happens and what happens because of it.

Sequence

First, next, then and finally can help us identify the order of events.

Cause and Effect

The cause tells us why something happened. The effect tells us what happened as a result.

Example

First, Kojo planted the seeds. Next, he watered them. After several days, the seeds began to grow.

Sequence: Plant → Water → Grow

Cause: Kojo watered the seeds.

Effect: The seeds received water and began to grow.

Reading Tip: Look for words such as first, next, later, because, so and therefore.
LESSON 6

Strategies for Better Reading

Reading strategies are methods that help readers understand a text before, during and after reading.

Preview

Look at the title, pictures and headings before reading.

Question

Ask questions about what you expect to learn.

Predict

Think about what may happen or what the text may discuss.

Summarise

After reading, explain the most important ideas briefly.

Try This

Before reading a passage about cocoa farming, look at the title and ask:

"What do I already know about cocoa farming?"

Then read the passage and check whether your ideas were correct.

Reading Tip: Good readers think before, during and after reading.
LESSON 7

Saying the Important Ideas Briefly

A summary gives the most important information from a text in a shorter form. A good summary does not include every small detail.

Step 1

Read the whole passage carefully.

Step 2

Identify the main idea and important details.

Step 3

Remove unnecessary information.

Step 4

Write the important ideas in your own words.

Example

Ama and her friends started a reading club at their community centre. They meet every Saturday to read stories and discuss new words.

Summary: Ama and her friends meet every Saturday to read and improve their reading skills.

Summary Tip: Ask yourself, "What are the two or three most important things I need to remember?"
LESSON 8

Reading Smoothly and Clearly

Reading fluency means reading accurately, smoothly and with appropriate expression. Fluent readers do not stop unnecessarily between words.

Accuracy

Read the words correctly.

Speed

Read at a comfortable pace without rushing.

Expression

Use your voice to show meaning and feeling.

Practise

Choose a short paragraph from the Reading Library. Read it silently first. Then read it aloud.

Pay attention to punctuation. Pause at commas and full stops and use expression when necessary.

Fluency Tip: Read the same passage more than once. Repeated reading can help you become smoother and more confident.
LESSON 9

Knowing Different Text Types

Different texts are written for different purposes. Knowing the type of text helps readers understand what to look for.

Story

Tells about characters, events and experiences.

Informational Text

Gives facts and information about a topic.

Instructions

Explain how to do something step by step.

Advertisement

Gives information about a product, service or event and tries to attract attention.

Example

"How to Plant a Tomato Seed" is an example of an instructional text because it gives steps for planting a seed.

A story about a girl visiting a Ghanaian market is a narrative or story text.

Reading Tip: Ask yourself, "Why did the writer create this text?"
